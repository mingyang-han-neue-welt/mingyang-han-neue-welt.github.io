The Motion-Staticity Research Program

The Motion-Staticity (MS) program is an inverse reconstruction approach. Instead of beginning with the Minkowski metric, Lorentz transformations, or a relativistic action as the primary starting point, it begins with a bounded state assigned to a massive process. The question is then: how much of familiar relativistic structure can be recovered when this state is required to possess mutually compatible operational and dynamical representations?

The primitive state contains a three-dimensional motion component $\mathbf D$ and a scalar staticity component $S$:

$$|\mathbf D|^2+S^2=1,\qquad S>0.$$

This equation should not be read as a spacetime metric. It is an internal Euclidean normalization of the state itself. Its physical meaning appears only after an operational realization is specified. A massive process is assumed to carry both a spatial displacement and an additive intrinsic scalar accumulation. The radial coefficient assigned by an inertial observer then defines an observer duration, and the bounded components become

$$\mathbf D=\frac{\mathbf v}{c},\qquad S=\frac{d\tau}{dt}.$$

From this starting point the papers develop in stages. The first paper studies the free-particle dynamics implied by compatibility with a canonical Hamiltonian description. The second separates the internal Lorentz generator space from the tangent space of a curved manifold and reconstructs the map relating them. The third asks what quantum kinematics arise if that reconstructed solder map itself becomes a configuration variable. The fourth follows a different, classical branch and asks how a locally reconstructed geometry acquires a global energy-momentum at an asymptotically flat boundary.

Paper I

From a bounded state to relativistic free-particle dynamics

The first paper establishes the flat-space foundation of the program. The key point is not simply that the relation $|\mathbf D|^2+S^2=1$ resembles the usual velocity/proper-time identity. The paper requires one and the same MS state to be represented in two different ways: operationally, through motion and proper-time rate, and canonically, through a free Hamiltonian $H(\mathbf p)$.

Hamilton's equation identifies the velocity with $\nabla_{\mathbf p}H$. The scalar part of the state is simultaneously required to agree with the corresponding canonical response. Combining the two channels gives the nonlinear consistency condition

$$\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbf p}H|^2}{c^2}+\frac{\left(H-\mathbf p\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf p}H\right)^2}{c^2Q^2}=1.$$

This equation alone does not automatically force the standard relativistic Hamiltonian. The paper explicitly exhibits degenerate lower-dimensional solutions. The strong rigidity result appears after imposing global conditions: the Hamiltonian is finite, convex and $C^1$, has the calibrated rest value $H(\mathbf0)=cQ$, and its canonical state map represents the complete massive MS state manifold. Under these assumptions,

$$H(\mathbf p)=c\sqrt{|\mathbf p|^2+Q^2}.$$

With $Q=mc$, this is the ordinary relativistic massive dispersion relation. Only after this dynamical result is obtained does the paper study covariance. The positive massive shell carries the connected Lorentz group as its maximal connected linear covariance group, and allowing freedom in the choice of event origin gives the corresponding proper orthochronous Poincaré structure.

Main role in the series: establish the relativistic massive-generator geometry that the later papers use as an input.
Paper II

From internal Lorentz geometry to curved spacetime

Once the first paper has produced an internal relativistic generator geometry, a new problem appears in curved spacetime. An internal four-dimensional Lorentz vector space and the tangent space of the spacetime manifold need not initially be identified. In standard tetrad language, that identification is supplied by a solder form. The second paper asks whether such a soldering relation can instead be reconstructed from operational information.

At each event $x$, the theory therefore starts with an internal Lorentz space $V_x$ and a tangent space $T_xM$ as distinct objects. The primitive information is a scalar generator-displacement response $\Theta_x(\Pi,\xi)$, where $\Pi$ is an internal massive-generator covector and $\xi$ is an infinitesimal spacetime displacement.

Additive composition, positive homogeneity, continuity, smooth dependence on the spacetime point, and operational faithfulness allow this response to extend uniquely to a bilinear pairing. Finite-dimensional duality then reconstructs a linear map

$$e_x:T_xM\longrightarrow V_x,\qquad \Theta_x(\Pi,\xi)=\Pi(e_x\xi).$$

Faithfulness makes this map injective, and because both spaces have rank four, it becomes an isomorphism. The internal Lorentz form $\eta$ can then be pulled back to spacetime:

$$g=e^*\eta.$$

An important distinction is calibration. If the response is known only up to a positive local scale, the construction fixes only the conformal metric class. Absolute response calibration is needed to obtain an absolute spacetime metric scale.

The paper also investigates gravity, but this is a separate downstream step. After introducing additional assumptions — including a torsion-free branch, metric ADM phase space, the standard Lorentzian hypersurface-deformation algebra, and a restricted form of the scalar constraint — off-shell closure selects the familiar ADM/Einstein branch. This is therefore a conditional gravitational completion, not a claim that Einstein's equations follow from the MS normalization alone.

Main role in the series: convert the internal Lorentz generator geometry of Paper I into a spacetime geometry through an operationally reconstructed solder map.
Paper III

Making the solder map into a quantum configuration variable

The third paper follows a quantum-kinematical branch rather than quantizing the gravitational completion of Paper II. Its starting point is the reconstructed solder map itself. At a fixed spacetime event, all linear solderings form the configuration space

$$\mathcal E_x=\operatorname{Hom}(T_xM,V_x).$$

The same scalar response used classically now becomes a family of configuration functions,

$$\Theta_{\Pi,\xi}(e)=\Pi(e\xi).$$

These functions are sufficiently rich to separate different soldering configurations. One might expect noncommutative quantum structure to emerge simply by representing the responses as operators, but the paper shows an important obstruction: if additive composition in the generator and displacement arguments is implemented exactly, the resulting response algebra is necessarily Abelian.

Noncommutativity therefore requires an additional physical principle. The paper assumes that translations of the soldering configuration $e\mapsto e+A$ can be implemented coherently. Introducing the standard cotangent phase space $T^*\mathcal E_x$, the generators of these deformations become conjugate to the response observables, producing

$$[\widehat K(A),\widehat\Theta_{\Pi,\xi}]=i\hbar\,\Pi(A\xi)I.$$

The response observables themselves still commute; the noncommutativity arises from operators that move between soldering configurations. After reduction by the internal Lorentz redundancy, the nondegenerate soldering variables reduce to the expected Lorentz metric configuration degrees of freedom.

A separate part of the paper studies finite rotational comparison and a possible spin-cover parity diagnostic. That result is deliberately narrow: it does not by itself produce spin-$1/2$ matter, Fermi statistics, a Standard Model gauge group, or a full quantum theory of gravity.

Main role in the series: identify a local quantum configuration and Weyl-type kinematics naturally associated with variations of the operationally reconstructed solder map.
Paper IV

From local geometry to the total momentum of an isolated system

The fourth paper returns to the classical branch. The previous construction gives local spacetime geometry and, under additional assumptions, bulk gravitational generators. But a local generally covariant theory does not automatically contain a well-defined total energy or momentum for an isolated gravitating system.

The paper therefore considers a calibrated asymptotically Minkowskian end satisfying the standard Regge-Teitelboim falloff and parity conditions. When the bulk generators are allowed to approach constant asymptotic translations, their variation develops a boundary term that prevents functional differentiability. Adding the appropriate boundary primitive completes the generator:

$$G[a]=G_0[a]+B_\infty[a].$$

Because $B_\infty[a]$ is linear in the asymptotic translation parameter $a$, it defines a translation covector $\mathsf P_\infty^{\mathrm{MS}}$. This is the point where a genuine global momentum-like object first appears in the MS construction.

The paper then expresses the resulting charge in observer space. The energy measured by an asymptotic observer $u$ is

$$E_\infty(u)=-\eta_\infty(P_\infty^{\mathrm{MS}},u).$$

In the future-timelike sector, minimizing this energy over observers gives the invariant radial mass. This mirrors the local construction in an interesting way: locally, one varies the massive generator while holding a displacement fixed to recover a proper-time norm; globally, one varies the observer while holding the total generator fixed to recover the invariant mass. The paper calls this relation local-global response reciprocity.

Finally, after imposing the empirical Einstein normalization $\kappa=8\pi G/c^4$, the completed translation charge agrees with the standard ADM four-momentum. The paper does not derive the numerical value of $G$, nor does it replace the standard positive-mass theorem.

Main role in the series: extend the locally reconstructed classical geometry to the asymptotic translation sector and identify the resulting global energy-momentum.
