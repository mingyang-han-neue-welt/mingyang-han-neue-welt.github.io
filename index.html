The Motion-Staticity Research Program

The Motion-Staticity (MS) program is an inverse reconstruction approach. Instead of beginning with the Minkowski metric, Lorentz transformations, or a relativistic action as the primary starting point, it begins with a bounded state assigned to a massive process. The basic question is how much familiar relativistic structure can be recovered when this state is required to possess compatible operational and dynamical representations.

The primitive state contains a three-dimensional motion component $\mathbf D$ and a scalar staticity component $S$:

$$|\mathbf D|^2+S^2=1,\qquad S>0.$$

This is an internal state-space normalization rather than an assumed spacetime metric. Once an operational realization is supplied, the components are identified as

$$\mathbf D=\frac{\mathbf v}{c},\qquad S=\frac{d\tau}{dt}.$$

The first stage of the program reconstructs relativistic free-particle dynamics and the massive Lorentz generator geometry. A second stage reconstructs the solder map relating this internal geometry to spacetime. From there the program branches. The classical branch studies gravitational constraints, torsion, asymptotic charges, stationary boundaries, Schwarzschild and Kerr response geometry, and positive energy. The quantum branch studies soldering deformations, projective comparison, possible spin fractionalization, relational multiplicity, internal comparison structure, and constitutive links between quantum comparison and Lorentzian Hodge geometry.

The closed-state papers add another layer. The massive open ball is completed by the projective null boundary $S=0$. This makes it possible to compare massive observer geometry, null directions, helicity bundles, Wigner transport and gravitational null-response tests on a common compact base. The papers repeatedly distinguish kinematical reconstruction from dynamical assumptions: Einstein equations, Newton's constant, the positive-mass theorem, Sen-Witten existence theory, Bondi flux, gauge-field propagation, and microscopic matter dynamics are not treated as automatic consequences of the compact-state relation.

Paper I

From a bounded state to relativistic free-particle dynamics

The first paper establishes the flat-space foundation of the program. The important requirement is that one and the same MS state admit both an operational description and a canonical Hamiltonian description. Hamilton's equation gives the velocity through $\nabla_{\mathbf p}H$, while the scalar part of the state is identified with the corresponding Legendre remainder.

Combining the vector and scalar channels gives

$$\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbf p}H|^2}{c^2}+\frac{\left(H-\mathbf p\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf p}H\right)^2}{c^2Q^2}=1.$$

The pointwise equation alone is not enough to determine the Hamiltonian: lower-dimensional degenerate solutions exist. Under global assumptions including finiteness, convexity, $C^1$ regularity, the calibrated rest value $H(\mathbf0)=cQ$, and complete representation of the massive MS state manifold, the nondegenerate solution is

$$H(\mathbf p)=c\sqrt{|\mathbf p|^2+Q^2}.$$

With $Q=mc$, this becomes the usual relativistic dispersion relation. The resulting positive massive shell then carries the connected Lorentz covariance structure, with translation-origin freedom giving the proper orthochronous Poincaré structure.

Main role in the series: establish the massive relativistic generator geometry used by the subsequent reconstruction.
Paper II

From internal Lorentz geometry to curved spacetime

The second paper separates the internal Lorentz generator space from the tangent space of spacetime. At an event $x$, the internal space $V_x$ and the tangent space $T_xM$ are initially distinct; no spacetime metric or solder form is assumed between them.

The primitive datum is a scalar generator-displacement response $\Theta_x(\Pi,\xi)$. Under composition, positive homogeneity, continuity, smooth base-point dependence and operational faithfulness, this response extends to a bilinear pairing and reconstructs

$$e_x:T_xM\longrightarrow V_x,\qquad \Theta_x(\Pi,\xi)=\Pi(e_x\xi).$$

Equal rank and faithfulness make the map an isomorphism, so the internal Lorentz form induces

$$g=e^*\eta.$$

An absolutely calibrated response determines the metric scale, whereas response known only up to positive local rescaling determines only a conformal class.

The paper also studies a restricted classical gravitational completion. The Einstein/ADM branch appears only after further assumptions such as a torsion-free branch, metric phase space, the standard hypersurface-deformation algebra and a restricted scalar constraint.

Main role in the series: reconstruct Lorentzian spacetime geometry from scalar operational generator-displacement incidences.
Paper III

Making the solder map into a quantum configuration variable

The third paper begins from the reconstructed solder map and asks what quantum kinematics becomes available when the map itself is allowed to vary. At one event the configuration space is

$$\mathcal E_x=\operatorname{Hom}(T_xM,V_x).$$

The operational responses become linear functions

$$\Theta_{\Pi,\xi}(e)=\Pi(e\xi).$$

Exact additive implementation of the response alone remains Abelian. Noncommutativity therefore requires an additional coherent-deformation principle $e\mapsto e+A$. With the cotangent completion, the deformation generators satisfy

$$[\widehat K(A),\widehat\Theta_{\Pi,\xi}]=i\hbar\,\Pi(A\xi)I.$$

Internal Lorentz reduction returns the expected metric configuration degrees of freedom. The paper also identifies a global projective spin-cover diagnostic, but does not derive matter, Fermi statistics or a gauge group.

Main role in the series: construct a local Weyl-type quantum kinematics for soldering configurations without claiming a quantization of general relativity.
Paper IV

From local response to gravitational charges at infinity and at a stationary horizon

The revised fourth paper makes the separation between geometry and gravitational dynamics particularly explicit. The local MS response supplies a calibrated soldered metric, but the torsion-free Einstein action and its coupling are taken as separate dynamical inputs. The question is then how standard gravitational charges appear once that response-derived geometry is placed in the Einstein Hamiltonian framework.

For an asymptotically Minkowskian Regge-Teitelboim phase space, a constant asymptotic translation produces the usual differentiability obstruction. The completed generator has the form

$$G[a]=G_0[\chi_a]+B_\infty[a],$$

and linearity of the boundary term defines the total translation covector $\mathsf P_\infty^{\mathrm{MS}}\in\mathfrak t_\infty^*$. Its observer energy reconstructs the associated Lorentz vector. When that vector is future timelike, its minimum over the future observer hyperboloid gives the invariant mass.

A new emphasis of the revised paper is asymptotic response calibration. At the orders visible to translation charges, the energy functional sees the monopole of the leading spatial scale, while the spatial translation coefficient sees the dipole of the normal first scale jet. These are only kinematic surface readouts: the gravitational constraints strongly restrict which such jets can actually occur in admissible data.

The global compact variables also admit a Hermitian representation. Once future causality is known, the normalized charge lies in the trace-one section of the positive Lorentz-Hermitian cone. This is an algebraic interface and is not used to assume positive mass.

After imposing $\kappa=8\pi G/c^4$, the asymptotic charge becomes the standard ADM four-momentum. The same Einstein normalization is then followed through minimally coupled particle sources and the covariant phase-space description of a stationary inner boundary. The horizon boost charge, first law, Wald area term and Komar-Smarr relation remain standard GR results; the MS contribution is their common organization in one calibrated response-derived metric.

Main role in the series: connect the MS-reconstructed metric to standard ADM translation charges, response calibration and stationary horizon charges while keeping the Einstein action and Newton's constant as explicit additional inputs.
Paper V

What can the null boundary determine about the massive interior?

This paper closes the MS hemisphere by adjoining $S=0$, whose projective image is the future null-direction sphere. It asks a natural inverse question: if the null boundary is known, does it determine the massive geometry inside?

For a compact convex state body $K$, the Hamiltonian is controlled by its support function:

$$H_K(p,Q)=c\,h_K(p,Q).$$

At zero intrinsic scale,

$$H_K(p,0)=c\,h_\Omega(p),\qquad \Omega=\operatorname{proj}_D K.$$

only the projected motion body remains. Distinct massive interiors can therefore possess the same projective null boundary and the same homogeneous boundary dispersion.

The paper then introduces the energy-staticity reciprocity defect

$$\mathcal R=hS-1,\qquad h=\frac{H}{cQ}.$$

Exact reciprocity, together with $C^2$ regularity at the unique rest state, forces

$$S^2=1-D^TAD,$$

so the compact state becomes an ellipsoid. Spatial isotropy and limiting speed calibration then recover the round Lorentzian state and the usual square-root Hamiltonian.

In a broader affine-sphere completion, derivatives of the reciprocity defect reconstruct the Pick cubic measuring nonquadratic anisotropy, while a renormalized boundary response is related to centro-affine curvature. The result is a hierarchy from universal projective boundary data to increasingly rigid bulk information.

Main role in the series: show precisely why a null boundary is insufficient by itself and identify additional response data capable of restoring bulk rigidity.
Paper VI

A conditional bosonic route to projective spin

This paper asks whether quantum comparison between different events may contain structure beyond the geometric Lorentz transport reconstructed from the soldering field. It introduces an independent comparison connection $A$ in addition to the geometric connection $\omega$ and studies their tensorial difference

$$C=A-\omega.$$

At low energies, the rotational part can provide bosonic $j=1$ comparison modes. When one such mode is bound to each occupied segment of a comparison string, neighboring segments decompose as

$$1\otimes1=0\oplus1\oplus2.$$

If the $J=2$ channel has the required positive stiffness, the effective chain approaches the AKLT/Haldane regime. An open string can then possess projective spin-$1/2$ endpoint modes even though every microscopic segment carries integer spin.

The mechanism is explicitly conditional. The independent comparison connection, the hierarchy of modes, occupation binding, the required channel ordering, deconfinement and spin-statistics are not derived from the original MS response axioms.

Main role in the series: turn the possible emergence of spinorial comparison sectors into explicit dynamical tests rather than assuming microscopic spinors from the start.
Paper VII

Relational multiplicity as an internal quantum degree of freedom

Starting from localized projective comparison carriers, the paper studies how many-body composition contains degrees of freedom invisible to the collective geometric action. For $N$ minimal carriers,

$$\mathcal H_N=\bigoplus_j V_j\otimes M_j.$$

The $V_j$ factor carries the total geometric spin, while the multiplicity space $M_j$ records the different ways the same spin sector can be assembled.

Different neutral pair projectors act noncommutatively on these multiplicity spaces, and small examples already generate complete qubit operator algebras. Quantum information may therefore survive entirely in relational degrees of freedom even when one-body geometric observables see no information.

When a protected multiplicity subspace varies over spacetime it becomes a vector bundle with an induced matrix-valued connection. Genuine curvature requires physical motion of the protected subspace, not merely a basis change. Fusion-compatible transport then gives a possible route toward a common internal comparison group.

Main role in the series: identify geometry-invisible relational multiplicities as candidate internal quantum degrees of freedom.
Paper VIII

From relational multiplicity to fusion-compatible internal structure

This paper develops the multiplicity construction further and focuses on the distinction between an available internal operator algebra, the symmetry of the effective dynamics and the holonomy group actually generated by transport. These structures need not coincide.

If $Z(x)$ is a local orthonormal frame for a protected relational subspace, the projected connection is

$$A=Z^\dagger DZ.$$

A changing basis in a fixed subspace gives only pure gauge. Nonzero curvature instead requires the physical projector defining the protected space to vary.

A common interaction structure requires more than separate bundles with isomorphic holonomies. If $C^{\,t}_{rs}:M_r\otimes M_s\to M_t$ describes a fusion channel, the transport is required to satisfy

$$U_\gamma^{(t)}C^{\,t}_{rs}=C^{\,t}_{rs}\left(U_\gamma^{(r)}\otimes U_\gamma^{(s)}\right).$$

This leads naturally to tensor-compatible internal comparison structure. A propagating Yang-Mills field remains a further infrared dynamical requirement, and no specific Standard Model group is derived.

Main role in the series: formulate the extra compatibility conditions needed to move from separate relational sectors toward a common internal symmetry.
Paper IX

Complex structure, four-dimensional rigidity and gravitational constitutive response

This paper investigates whether the complex structure occurring in quantum comparison can be related, without literally identifying the two, to the Hodge structure of Lorentzian gravitational response.

In the Lorentz bivector representation, requiring a real Lorentz-equivariant complex structure selects the four-dimensional case, where

$$\operatorname{End}_{SO^+(1,3)}(\Lambda^2V)=\mathbb RI\oplus\mathbb R\star.$$

The only invariant real complex structures in this representation are $\pm\star$. A separate boundary argument reaches the same dimension under a rank-one complex helicity-line assumption.

The same commutant restricts a local Lorentz-equivariant gravitational constitutive law to

$$C=\alpha I+\beta\star.$$

An additional graph-compatibility condition can select the pure Hodge direction, while the overall magnitude remains an undetermined gravitational stiffness.

The paper also studies how quantum spin sources can be transferred through a classical constitutive response to produce operator-valued geometric correlations. This source-induced quantum geometry is explicitly distinguished from an intrinsic quantum theory of gravity.

Main role in the series: connect quantum-comparison complex structure, four-dimensional Hodge geometry and gravitational constitutive response under clearly stated additional assumptions.
Paper X

Torsion as an operational closed-response observable

This paper revisits the torsion-free condition used in the earlier gravitational branch. Instead of imposing zero torsion directly, it constructs a covariant response phase around a small closed loop.

For $\gamma=\partial\Sigma$ of size $\ell$,

$$\log W_\gamma(P)=-\frac{i}{\hbar}\int_\Sigma P_aT^a+O(\ell^3).$$

Since the massive shell spans the internal dual space, disappearance of the area-order phase for all shell generators implies $T^a=0$. Torsion freedom can therefore be expressed as an operational loop-closure condition.

The solder map also supplies simple area bivectors

$$B^{ab}=e^a\wedge e^b.$$

A local linear Lorentz-equivariant constitutive relation between these areas and gravitational excitation must have the form

$$H=C(B),\qquad C=\alpha I+\beta\star.$$

This contains the Einstein-Cartan/Palatini and Holst directions. Neither Newton's constant nor the relative Holst coefficient is fixed by the operational kinematics alone.

Main role in the series: give torsion a direct response-loop interpretation and locate the constitutive boundary between reconstructed geometry and additional gravitational physics.
Paper XI

Schwarzschild and Kerr through stationary response spectra

This paper applies the response-polar geometry to stationary and axisymmetric spacetimes. For commuting stationary and axial Killing fields $k$ and $m$, it introduces

$$R_x(\Omega)=-g_x(k+\Omega m,k+\Omega m).$$

Since this function is quadratic in $\Omega$, its spectrum reconstructs the full Killing-plane Gram matrix and therefore the local stationary redshift, frame dragging and axial geometry.

A static-limit surface satisfies $R(0)=0$, whereas a regular non-axis Killing horizon occurs when the two null angular-velocity roots coalesce:

$$R(\Omega_H)=0,\qquad \partial_\Omega R(\Omega_H)=0.$$

In Schwarzschild the two degeneracies coincide; in Kerr they separate, allowing the response spectrum to distinguish the ergosurface, local dragging and horizon angular velocity.

In the stationary axisymmetric vacuum sector, derivatives of the response recover the twist data leading to the Ernst potential. At the global level the calibrated Killing fields reproduce the standard Komar charges and organize the Smarr relation. These GR structures are used with their standard status rather than rederived from the compact MS relation.

Main role in the series: demonstrate how the response language organizes familiar stationary Schwarzschild/Kerr geometry from local observer data to global charges.
Paper XII

Spin, helicity and topology on the closed MS ball

This paper adds spin representation theory to the closed MS state space. At fixed intrinsic scale $Q>0$, massive momentum space is compactified into the open motion ball

$$D=\frac{p}{\sqrt{Q^2+|p|^2}},\qquad S=\frac{Q}{\sqrt{Q^2+|p|^2}},\qquad |D|^2+S^2=1.$$

The center is the isotropic rest state, while $S=0$ is the projective null boundary. A useful equivalent representation is

$$\rho(D)=\frac12(1+D\cdot\sigma),\qquad \det\rho=\frac{S^2}{4}.$$

The interior consists of positive rank-two Hermitian matrices; the boundary consists of rank-one projectors and is naturally $\mathbb{CP}^1\simeq S^2$.

Away from rest, the direction $n=D/|D|$ allows a massive spin-$j$ representation to be decomposed into directional helicity lines $L_h$. Their first Chern classes are

$$c_1(L_h)=-2h.$$

A nonzero-helicity line therefore cannot be smoothly extended through the directionless rest point. The full spin multiplet remains trivial because its Chern classes cancel. The obstruction can be resolved geometrically by blowing up the rest point into its sphere of approach directions.

The outer boundary has a different obstruction. A rank-$2j+1$ massive carrier cannot become a rank-one fixed-helicity carrier simply by smooth restriction; a projection, quotient or other reduction is necessary. The parameter $S$ simultaneously controls the familiar $SO(3)\rightarrow E(2)$ little-group contraction and the flattening of constant-staticity direction spheres.

The massive Wigner-Thomas connection is identified with the canonical $SU(2)$ connection on $SL(2,\mathbb C)/SU(2)$. In a helicity frame its off-diagonal mixing terms diverge like $S^{-1}$, but projecting to a fixed helicity line before taking the boundary limit leaves the finite Berry curvature

$$f_h=-h\,d\Omega_2.$$

Finally, Borel-Weil reconstruction shows that although a boundary extreme-helicity fiber is one-dimensional, the global holomorphic sections of the corresponding line recover the full spin-$j$ representation.

Main role in the series: place massive spin, helicity bundles, rest-state topology, little-group contraction and Wigner transport on one closed MS base while keeping a projective null ray distinct from a finite-energy massless particle.
Paper XIII

Null responses, positive energy and the Sen–Witten lift

This paper connects the closed projective boundary to the positive-energy problem for gravitational translation charges. A total translation charge $P=(E,p)$ defines, for each projective null direction $n$, the scalar response

$$F_P(n)=E-p\cdot n.$$

Writing the same charge as the Hermitian matrix

$$\mathbf P=E\,1-p\cdot\sigma,$$

gives a simple cone-duality statement: positivity of $F_P(n)$ for every null direction is equivalent to $\mathbf P\ge0$, and hence to $E\ge|p|$.

The paper goes beyond this elementary cone statement by asking which angular response functions really correspond to one four-dimensional translation. A null-to-timelike Poisson transform reconstructs observer energy, but its scalar equation alone admits more angular modes. The stronger tensor equation

$$\operatorname{Hess}_{H}E=E\,g_H$$

selects precisely the $\ell=0,1$ translation sector. A trace-free spherical Hessian therefore provides a quantitative measure of how far a general angular response lies from a genuine translation response.

The invariant mass can also be recovered from the variance of null responses seen by an observer:

$$-\eta(P,P)=E_P(U)^2-3\,\operatorname{Var}_{\mu_U}\!\left(\frac{F_P}{A_U}\right).$$

In Einstein initial data, explicit positive-response identities are followed by the established Sen-Witten construction. Polarizing the Sen-Witten solution in two independent asymptotic spinor inputs produces a matrix Gram factorization of the positive translation form,

$$\mathbf P=T^\dagger T.$$

Its determinant gives the invariant mass and simultaneously defines a Hermitian metric on the boundary spinor line. The same response form lifts through symmetric powers to the higher-spin coherent kernels and Borel-Weil section norms.

The paper also incorporates the standard Bondi flux law. Radiation is represented as a positive collection of null channels, leading to a finite staticity decomposition and the exact differential balance

$$\dot q_B=-\int_{S^2}|\nu-D_B|^2\,L\,d\Omega\le0.$$

This is a response reformulation of standard Bondi dynamics, not a derivation of the flux law from MS kinematics.

The final quantum analysis is deliberately cautious. A specified positive kinetic shell preserves useful staticity bounds, even in a finite-dimensional noncommuting extension. But positivity for every classical null direction does not in general imply positivity of the full operator-valued Hermitian block. Full Weyl-shift covariance is also incompatible with semibounded energy, and classical positive squares can be destroyed by operator ordering. A physical quantum ADM theory would therefore require constraint reduction, a physical Hilbert space, asymptotic generators, covariance and additional joint positivity structure.

Main role in the series: connect projective null probes, ADM positive energy, Sen-Witten spinorial lifting, boundary Chern geometry, Bondi radiation and quantum positivity constraints without claiming a new general positive-mass theorem or a quantum ADM Hamiltonian.
